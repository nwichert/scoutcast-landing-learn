## Maker comment (post within 60 seconds of launch — 380 words)

I built Scoutcast because every morning I'd open my phone, open ESPN, close it, open Twitter, close it, open Reddit, close it, and twenty minutes later I still didn't know what happened to my teams overnight. The information existed. It was just scattered across nine apps, each optimized to keep me scrolling, none optimized to actually tell me what I needed to know.

So I asked a simple question: what if sports media worked like a friend who'd already done the reading? Two minutes. The Bucks lost. Giannis went off in the third. Here's the trade rumor everyone's talking about. Done.

That's what Scoutcast does. You pick your teams, your leagues, and a few X writers you actually trust. Every morning, it generates a roughly two-minute audio briefing of your teams, your storylines, your sources. If something catches your ear, you tap Ask: "what's his stat line tonight?" or "more on the trade." The briefing pauses, answers in your headphones, and picks up where it left off. Hands stay on the dumbbell or the stroller.

Who it's for: people who used to be obsessive sports fans but life got in the way. Commuters. Parents. Fantasy players who don't want to read three Reddit threads to set a lineup. People who like sports media but hate sports media's attention economy.

What it doesn't do yet (the honest part): we're Apple-only for now (iOS, iPadOS, macOS). Android is on the roadmap but isn't built. Live in-game commentary isn't here either; this is morning catch-up, not play-by-play. And our X writer integration depends on whoever is still actually posting on X.

It's free forever. The one paid thing is an NFL Fantasy Season Pass for $49.99 per season if you want lineup-aware briefings synced to your Yahoo, ESPN, Sleeper, or NFL.com leagues.

I'd love your honest take on the audio length. We hover around two minutes because that's a stoplight, a kettle, an elevator. Some folks want 90 seconds, others want five. What's your morning sports window, and what would make you replace your current habit?

## Rules used to write this kit (so future edits stay consistent)

- Taglines: max 60 chars, no period at end, no hashtags, do not start with "Scoutcast", no superlatives, not a question
- Description: max 500 chars, third person or imperative voice, no links, no "I"
- Maker comment: 300–400 words, first person, opens with real origin story, includes honest caveat, ends with specific question
- Tweets: max 280 chars each, no hashtags
- LinkedIn: 150–300 words, professional but not stiff
- Emails: under 150 words each, subject under 50 chars
- Banned words across all assets: revolutionary, game-changing, powerful, robust, seamless, innovative, best-in-class, streamline
- No em dashes anywhere
- No competitor names anywhere (despite the on-site comparator)
